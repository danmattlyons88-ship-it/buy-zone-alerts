FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How the tools work — and what they are (and aren't) for.

How does the Leverage Calculator work?
It multiplies your estimated underlying price move by your effective leverage. Example: a 2% move at 10x leverage models a 20% gain on your capital. Enter your capital invested, the expected % increase in the underlying, and your leverage multiple — the tool estimates your dollar return, percentage gain, and projected account value. Remember: leverage cuts both ways. The same math applies to losses, and options can lose value even when the underlying moves in your direction (time decay, IV crush).
How do I read the RSI Buy Zone signal?
RSI (Relative Strength Index) measures momentum on a 0–100 scale. A reading of 30 or below is traditionally considered oversold — our "Buy Zone" — meaning selling pressure may be exhausted. A reading of 70 or above is considered overbought — the "Sell Zone." Anything between is Neutral. RSI is one indicator, not a guarantee: assets can stay oversold or overbought for a long time, so combine it with price action, volume, and your own risk plan.
Where do I find an asset's current RSI value?
Most free charting platforms show RSI. On TradingView, open any chart, click "Indicators," and search "RSI" (default 14-period). Webull and Robinhood also display RSI in their charting tools, and most crypto exchanges like Kraken Pro and Coinbase Advanced include it. The daily (1D) timeframe RSI is the most common reference for swing-trade buy zones.
How are the +20% / +50% / +100% profit targets calculated?
They're simple multiples of the current price you enter: price × 1.2, × 1.5, and × 2.0. They're meant as quick reference levels for planning exits and scaling out of positions — not predictions that the asset will reach them.
