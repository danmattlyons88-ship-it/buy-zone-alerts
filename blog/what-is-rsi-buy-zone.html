What Is RSI and Why Does 30 Matter?

If you've messed with the Buy Zone calculator on this site, you've seen "RSI" pop up. Here's what it actually is, in plain terms — no jargon.

RSI, in one sentence

RSI is just a number between 0 and 100 that tells you how hard and how fast a stock or crypto has been getting bought or sold lately. That's it. It doesn't predict the future — it just measures recent momentum.

Why everyone watches 30 and 70

When RSI drops under 30, it means something's been sold off hard and fast — probably harder than it "should" have been. Traders call that oversold, and that's your BUY ZONE signal on this site. When RSI climbs above 70, it means the opposite — people have been buying it up fast, and it might be due for a breather. That's the SELL ZONE.

Heads up: oversold doesn't mean "guaranteed bounce." A stock can stay oversold for a while, especially if something's genuinely wrong with it. RSI tells you momentum, not the future.

How I actually use it

I don't buy just because RSI hit 30. I use it as a heads-up — "okay, this is worth a closer look." Then I check the chart, see what's going on with the news, and decide from there. RSI is one piece of the puzzle, not the whole picture.

Bottom line

RSI under 30 = possible buy zone. RSI over 70 = possible sell zone. Everything in between is just normal noise. Plug your number into the calculator and let it do the math for you.
